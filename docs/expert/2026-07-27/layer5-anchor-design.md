# layer5-anchor-design (agent report, 2026-07-26 pre-flight sweep)

## Summary

Anchored canonization of 5-row C=6 states via missing-pair invariants is strongly feasible: on 600 random states the generic column-signature argmin explores a mean of 171.3 (box,flip) candidates while the anchored search explores 17.5 (box anchor only) or 7.0 (box+flip anchor) — a 9.8x-24.5x reduction in explored candidates, and 3.6x-6.2x after charging invariant-computation overhead under a conservative cell-op model (Python wall clock: 4.0x). One WL round over missing-pair edge invariants makes the box order unique in 99.3% of states, and in 46.8% of states the full candidate set is a single group element. All 300 random-group-element consistency checks pass. A directly analogous anchor works for 4-row states (4-regular missing-box multigraph + agreement invariants: 97% unique box order, 10.7x eval reduction). Prototypes: scratchpad\anchor5_proto.py and scratchpad\anchor5_flips.py.

Generic baseline: column-signature argmin refinement — at depth d, every remaining (box, flip) candidate is scored by the sorted multiset of (d+1)-column row projections; argmin survivors extend. Cost metric per the task: explored candidates.

KEY STRUCTURAL FINDING. The generic search is maximally degenerate at depth 0: every single column of a 5-row state has exactly 2 zeros, 5 side-0, 5 side-1 entries (each row fills both slots of every box), so all 12 depth-0 candidates tie, forcing 12 nodes x 10 candidates = 120 evaluations at depth 1. Generic mean = 171.3 evals (min 152, med 166, p90 192, max 242); even discounting the free depth-0 level, 159.3. This degeneracy is exactly what the missing-pair anchor removes.

ANCHOR INVARIANTS (all G-invariant: side flips preserve per-box agreement, box permutations permute them). Per box b with missing pair {x,y}: L1 = a_b = #{c!=b : x,y agree at c}. Edge invariant E(b,c) = (pair-of-b agrees at c?, pair-of-c agrees at b?, unordered pair of cross-agreement multisets between the two pairs over the 4 common boxes). L2 = 1 WL round over (a, E); L3 = 2 rounds. Flip anchor: for box b, compare sorted multisets of missed-box colors of the 5 symbols on side 0 vs side 1; if unequal, the flip is forced.

DISCRETIZATION (600 states; N_orders = prod k_i! over color classes): L1 alone: never unique — hist {2:32, 4:105, 6:133, 8:70, 12:132, 24:53, 36:3, 48:59, 120:10, 720:3}. L2: unique in 99.3% (596/600; rest N_orders=2). L3: 99.5% (597/600). So one WL round essentially settles the box order; L2 and L3 are equivalent in practice. Flip anchor fixes all 6 flips in 46.8% of states, >=5 boxes in 84.7%, >=4 in 97.7% (hist {2:1, 3:13, 4:78, 5:227, 6:281}). Joint: in 46.8% of states the anchor-consistent candidate set is exactly ONE group element out of 46,080.

COST COMPARISON (candidate evaluations, mean [min/med/p90/max]): generic 171.3 [152/166/192/242]; anchored L3 (box order only) 17.5 [14/16/20/36]; anchored L1 37.6 [16/32/68/160]; anchored L3 + flip anchor 7.0 [6/7/9/18]. Per-state ratio generic/anchored-L3: min 4.78, med 10.0, mean 10.05, max 16.4 — 100% of states exceed 1.5x. Final-depth survivor count is 1 for 599/600 states (max 2), so automorphism blowup is a non-issue on random states.

OVERHEAD-ADJUSTED SPEEDUP. Invariant cost counted as raw cell comparisons: L1 = 30, L3 = 570 (30 ordered box pairs x 18) ~ 20.8 generic-eval-equivalents at the measured 27.4 cell-ops/eval. Conservative estimates: evals+overhead: L3 4.48x, L3+flip-anchor 6.2x, L1 4.43x; weighted cell-op model: 3.76x (L3), 3.56x (L1); Python wall clock (includes real sorting/hashing constants): generic 603us vs anchored-L3+invariants 149us = 4.03x. Optimistic bound (eval counts only, i.e. if invariants are amortized or made negligible with 12-bit XOR/popcount agreement kernels — ~60 popcounts per state via the 15-box-pair cross-agreement table): 9.8x (L3), 24.5x (L3+flips). Note L1 is nearly free (30 comparisons) and alone already yields ~4.4x; the WL edge invariants buy the rest and are the only material overhead.

CORRECTNESS. Anchored canonical form = argmin restricted to color-sorted box orders (and allowed flips); the candidate set is defined purely by G-invariant data, so it is a valid canonical form. Empirically: apply random g in G, recompute invariants, recanonize — anchored L3: 120/120 equal; anchored L1: 60/60; flip-anchored: 120/120; generic: 30/30; 4-row anchored: 60/60. Zero failures across 390 checks.

4-ROW ANALOG (500 states). Structure confirmed: each symbol misses exactly 2 boxes, each box missed by exactly 4 symbols — a 4-regular multigraph on 6 boxes with 12 edges. Edge-multiplicity profile alone is weak (N_orders hist {4:11, 8:42, 24:82, 48:281, 720:84} — never unique) because random multigraphs here are nearly multiplicity-regular. Adding within-edge and within-vertex agreement invariants + 2 WL rounds: unique box order in 97.0% (485/500, rest 2 or 4). Generic 163.2 evals vs anchored 15.2 = 10.7x, consistency 60/60. So yes, the anchor generalizes, but it needs the agreement invariants, not just the multigraph shape.

CAVEATS. (1) Sampling: random backtracking states approximate, not T_5-weighted DP-visited states; highly symmetric states (where anchors degrade to the generic fallback) may have different measure, but both methods degrade together there and the tie fallback reuses the same argmin machinery. (2) The generic baseline is the specified column-signature argmin; a hand-tuned generic that structurally skips depth-0 still costs 159 evals (the depth-1 blowup is inherent). (3) Cost-proportional-to-candidates is the task's stated model; real constants shift the answer within the 3.6x-24.5x bracket, with wall-clock 4.0x as the honest midpoint indicator.

## Verdict

YES — a 1.5x+ speedup is not just plausible but conservative; every measured model clears it with margin (worst per-state ratio observed: 4.78x; most conservative overhead-charged aggregate: 3.6x; Python wall clock: 4.0x; headroom to ~6-24x with flip anchoring and popcount-based invariant kernels). Recommended configuration: L3 colors (missing-pair edge invariants + 2 WL rounds, though 1 round suffices) plus the flip anchor, with the generic argmin as fallback on residual ties (~0.5% of states for box order). Implementation risk is LOW-MODERATE: the invariants verified G-invariant on 390 random-group-element checks; the two real risks are (a) the anchored form is a different canonical representative, so the entire 5-row level must switch atomically — safe because level keys are independent and N(C) checksums (all 355 C=5 triples, M_3 = 12,324,872) are canonical-form-independent and should be re-verified after the switch; (b) invariant overhead engineering — it must be implemented with 12-bit XOR/popcount agreement tables (~60 popcounts/state) or amortized incrementally across the ~emissions of a shared 4-row parent, else the conservative 3.6x is the ceiling rather than the floor. The 4-row analog exists and works (missing-box multigraph + agreement invariants: 97% unique box order, 10.7x eval reduction) but plain edge multiplicities are insufficient; pursue it only if 3->4 canonization volume warrants, since the 2.4e12-canonization 4->5 bucket is where the ~4x wall-clock win lands.
